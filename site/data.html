Your brief, your key_

A project that asks you to trust an open bar owes you the boring details: what leaves your machine, what we keep, for how long, and what we never keep at all.

what travels where
what where it goes what we keep
The brief text To our service, then to the model provider that judges it — Anthropic by default, or OpenRouter when you bring a key. never stored
The verdicts Back to you. Each carries a short quote copied verbatim from your brief as evidence. cached 24h — keyed by a hash of the brief, so the same brief is not re-judged and re-charged. Send no_cache: true and nothing is written.
Your model key Forwarded once, over TLS, to the provider — as the Authorization header of that single call. never stored, never logged, never part of any cache key
Who you are Nowhere. There are no accounts, no cookies, no analytics on these pages. nothing to keep

The cache exists for one reason: the judge is deterministic at temperature 0, so scoring the same brief twice should cost once and answer identically. It holds verdicts — status, confidence, a short quote — never the brief itself, and it expires in a day.

bring your own key

With your key, we run no model for you.

Send an OpenRouter key in the x-llm-key header and the judgement runs on your account, against any model you choose. We forward the call and hand you the result — no model of ours is invoked, and nothing about the key is retained.

bash · your own judge
curl -X POST https://briefs.welance.com/v1/score \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -H 'x-llm-key: sk-or-…' \
  -d '{"brief":"…","judge":"llm","model":"anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.6","no_cache":true}'

The MCP server reads the same key from PB_LLM_KEY, so an assistant on your machine can score briefs entirely on your own account.

if that is not enough

Some briefs cannot leave a perimeter, and some organisations need a data-processing agreement, a named model, or the whole thing running somewhere else. That is a conversation, not a checkbox: write to hello@welance.com and we will tell you plainly what is possible — including running it on models we select, or handing you the deployment to run yourself. It is MIT-licensed; self-hosting is a right, not a favour.

the honest limits

We do not control the provider's own retention: once a brief reaches Anthropic or OpenRouter, their terms apply, and you should read them if the content is sensitive. The public service is rate-limited per IP and served over TLS, but it is a small open-source service, not a compliance product. For anything genuinely confidential, bring your own key, send no_cache, or run your own copy.
