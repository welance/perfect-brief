the method

The welance method_

The process we have run in person since 2012 — take an honest brief, price it transparently, assemble the team around it — digitalised end to end and opened as a blueprint. In an era when AI makes producing ever cheaper, what decides is composition: who you put together, and how fast you learn whether it works.

the problem, honestly

The big consulting firms sell teams by the day, at full list price, staffed by whoever was on the bench — a model that lives off utilisation, not fit. It optimises economic capital and structurally ignores the human and relational kind: the people doing the work are interchangeable by design, and the client relationship is kept proprietary to the firm — hidden behind it — instead of belonging to the humans who actually build together. It is an old model, and an unsustainable one; the wasted budget is priced in.

A cross-functional team assembled for the goal — people personally and professionally inclined to exactly this problem, not adapted to it — does better work. That is not a slogan; it is the one consistent pattern in fifteen years of engagements in the segment we serve: bounded, product-shaped work, where fit beats scale.

Organizations which design systems … are constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures of these organizations.

Melvin E. Conway, How Do Committees Invent? (1968) — made famous by Fred Brooks in The Mythical Man-Month as “Conway’s Law”.

You ship your team’s communication structure. Composing the team is not staffing, it is product design — which is why who works with whom is the decision we treat as the most important one.

the method — three steps
step 1

Perfect Brief

What the work is. A great product is 20% brief and 80% team — but the brief is the 20% that decides whether a good team can even start well. Scored against public rules, gated before it publishes.

→ score a brief
step 2

Perfect Price

What it is worth, and who gets what part. The share follows from how much of the role someone covers — never from a judgement about who they are. Stated so anyone can compute and check the split.

→ compute a split
step 3

Perfect Team

Who does it, where in the world, at one blended rate the client can actually reason about. Margins vary inside the team by level; the project clears a stated welance margin; nobody is squeezed below their rate.

→ assemble a team

Each step needs the one before it. The price needs the brief's scope to know what a role even is; the team needs the price's arithmetic to give three people one honest rate. Information cascades — that is why they are steps and not features, and why the brief stays the principal concept: nothing downstream survives a dishonest one.

welance — we do it with you

Given an engagement, welance finds, contacts and assembles the team, and answers for the delivery. That work is priced by the split you can read on this site — 70/30 at full autonomy — and guarded by the no-deal rule: we would rather lose an engagement than compress a person below their rate. The opposite of a day rate for whoever was available.

what the formulas cannot see

Si parte! The formulas produce the starting team, and then work begins for real — not a trial, not a pilot: the actual work. What no formula can see is learned only by collaborating: cultural compatibility between members and with the client, kindness, humanity, availability, how someone carries stress, the whole mass of soft skills that only show up when the pressure is real.

the fine print

So the start carries a declared reserve: scheduled checks at one to three days, weeks, or months — scaled to the project's size and complexity — where either side may say it is not working, and the initial choices can be replaced, removed, or modified. Bidirectional, always: the independent may not feel right either, and every human problem should surface at the first check rather than fester. That early surfacing is what prevents resentment, bad vibes, and ugly projects.

In white-collar work, ideas, intuition and mental elasticity need room to convert into success. When something is off, the adjustment is made on the most important asset: the people.

why open

This is what we have always done as an agency, in person, engagement by engagement. Digitalising it and opening it turns a practice into a blueprint: open rules for the brief, one readable file for the price, one equation for the team — MIT like the rest, so a collective that disagrees with our numbers can fork the method and run its own. We open the model; we curate the membership. That curation is the service, and saying so plainly is part of the transparency.

A collective priced this way is resilient the way a network is resilient: no bench to feed, no fixed shape to break, a declared reserve instead of a hidden one. The claim stays small and true: a brief you can score, a split you can compute, a team you can reason about — and an honest check, early, where the humans decide what the formulas cannot.
