The MCP connector (/mcp)

  • What it receives. The arguments your AI client sends to a tool: the brief text, your model's verdicts, the ruleset version and a few switches. Three tools take no input at all.
  • What it does with them. Computes the answer in memory and sends it back. It calls no language model, makes no other network request, and writes nothing: no database, no cache, no log of the brief or the verdicts.
  • Who judges. Your own AI client's model, under your provider's terms (for example Anthropic for Claude, OpenAI for ChatGPT). We never see that conversation, only the tool calls.

The local server you install with perfect-brief-mcp sends the same arguments to the same free routes (/v1/byo/*), which behave the same way.

The scoring API (/v1)

  • What it receives. The brief text and its options. Text only, never files: a document you open in the console is read in your browser.
  • The model judge. When a request asks for the model judge (the console's AI judge, the welance/directory, or any caller of /v1/score and /v1/suggest), the brief is sent to OpenRouter, which passes it to the model provider for that call. The models a request may use are listed at /v1/models. Once a brief leaves us, OpenRouter's and that provider's terms apply.
  • Your own key. An OpenRouter key sent in x-llm-key is used for that one call and is never stored or logged.
  • The keyword judge and the free /v1/byo/* routes call no model.

What is kept, and for how long

  • The brief itself: not stored.
  • Model verdicts and suggestions: cached for 24 hours so the same brief is not judged twice. The cache key is a SHA-256 hash of the brief, with the ruleset version and model. A verdict holds the rule, the result, a confidence and a short quote copied from the brief as evidence; a suggestion holds the proposed sentence. Nothing is cached when you send your own key, and a score request with no_cache: true skips the cache. The MCP connector caches nothing.
  • Rate limits: one counter per IP address (or per X-API-Key value), stored as a SHA-256 hash, deleted when its one-minute window ends.
  • Server logs: our web server and hosting platform record each request's IP address, time, method, path and status. Model calls are logged with the model, status and duration. Brief text and keys are never written to the logs.

In your browser

No cookies and no analytics. The pages remember your theme and language in your browser's local storage, and the landing page hands a brief you typed to the console the same way; the console deletes it on reading. The pages load fonts from welance.com, and the console also loads fonts from Google Fonts, so those servers see your IP address.

What we do not do

We do not sell or share what you send, we do not train models on it, and we do not contact anyone because of what a brief said. The Operator Covenant binds welance to this. There are no accounts and nothing to sign up for.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you may ask what we hold about you, and have it corrected or deleted; you may also complain to a data protection authority. As we keep no accounts and no briefs, there is usually nothing to find beyond short-lived cache entries and server logs. Write to hello@welance.com. To stop using the connector, remove it from your AI client.
