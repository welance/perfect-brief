# A brief that supports an informed discussion

Date: 2026-10-06

Status: Accepted product direction. Changes to scoring criteria remain proposals
subject to evidence and the existing rule-change governance.

## Decision

A perfect brief makes expectations explicit and open to discussion, so that
client and supplier can understand whether and how to work together before
committing. Its purpose is to help demand and supply meet with a shared
understanding of the requested outcome and the decisions still to make.

This defines the intended quality of the artifact; it does not rename The Brief
Bar or assert that the current score already measures every part of that quality.

The brief should make six questions easy to answer:

1. What outcome is wanted, for whom, and why?
2. What is expected from the supplier, and what will the client provide or decide?
3. What has been decided, on what basis, and what remains a preference or proposal?
4. What resources and time are available, and how firm are those boundaries?
5. What uncertainties, alternatives or tradeoffs need discussion?
6. How will the parties recognise an acceptable result?

These are reading questions, not six new scored rules or mandatory sections.
The level of detail should fit the engagement being requested. A discovery brief
can define a useful research outcome before a delivery solution or price exists.

## Expectations and uncertainty

A well-defined unknown names what is unresolved, who can help resolve it, and
which decision depends on it. An agreed quote, final architecture or supplier
estimate is not a prerequisite for a good brief. Unresolved matters should be
visible rather than filled with invented certainty to earn points.

An amount needs a meaning: available budget, ceiling, initial estimate or agreed
price, and the work it covers. A date needs its status: binding deadline,
preferred target or planning assumption. A technical choice needs its role:
existing dependency, required constraint, preference or option for the supplier
to evaluate. Equivalent plain language is sufficient; no taxonomy is required.

Fixed scope and justified constraints are legitimate. A brief need not promise
cuts or flexibility it does not have. When a conflict is known or suspected,
the brief should expose the question and the next decision, including the
possibility that a supplier cannot take the work on those terms.

Useful detail can remain in attachments, provided the main brief makes the
requested engagement and the status of those attachments clear. Length alone
does not establish quality or suitability.

## What the score can support

The intended signal is how clearly the text supports an informed discussion.
It cannot certify the client's understanding, supplier agreement, feasibility,
legal compliance or a successful future relationship. Textual evidence can
identify ambiguity and conflicting expectations; claims about actual cost or
delivery capability need evidence beyond the brief.

Directory publication policy remains distinct from general brief quality.
Anonymity and a marketplace budget floor do not define a good brief in every
context. Deterministic code continues to own weights, aggregation and publication.

## Consequences

Use the [audit of all fourteen rules](../critique/expectations-rule-audit.md) to
test the current criteria before adding or reweighting rules. Sharpen an existing
rule when its purpose already covers the gap. Keep product direction, candidate
criteria and measured results visibly distinct.

A criterion change needs controlled examples, legitimate counterexamples,
human-labelled expectations and evaluation of the actual judge. A mock regression
alone cannot establish semantic validity. The existing
[contribution process](../../CONTRIBUTING.md) and
[governance](../../GOVERNANCE.md) continue to apply.
